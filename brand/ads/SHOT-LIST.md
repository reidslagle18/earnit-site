# EarnIt shot list — everything shootable on an iPhone

Two sets, because paid ads and brand surfaces have different jobs. Both are
shot on your phone, near a window, midday. No flash, no staging, no people's
faces required.

---

## Set A — the paid-acquisition shots (do these first)

The Meta Ad Library shows a clean split: brands whose product people find hard
to *believe* run deliberately un-designed, organic-looking statics, while
deposit and investing brands run polished branded design. EarnIt is squarely in
the first group — a parent has to believe this actually works on their kid's
phone before anything else matters.

Rocket Money's standout static is a photograph, shot slightly tilted, of a
laptop on a kitchen counter showing a real $279.88 phone bill, with a
**neon-green Post-it stuck to the screen** carrying a handwritten note. No
logo, no brand color, no designed type. It reads as a photo a friend texted
you. That is the format to copy.

**Shoot these:**

1. **The Post-it note.** Your iPhone propped on a kitchen counter showing the
   EarnIt task list with the LOCKED state visible. Stick a neon Post-it on the
   screen, handwritten in marker. Shoot slightly tilted, phone in hand, not
   square-on. Write one of these by hand:
   - *"My 11yo makes his bed now. Without me asking. Took about 3 days."*
   - *"Screen time used to be a fight every night. Now it's just a list."*
   - *"He cleaned the whole kitchen to get Roblox back. I'm not even mad."*
2. **The same shot, three different notes.** Same setup, swap the Post-it.
   Cheapest way to get three testable variants from one setup.
3. **The locked home screen.** Your kid's phone (or yours) showing a home
   screen with apps visibly greyed/locked. Shot handheld, on a real surface.
4. **The counter scene.** Phone face-down on the counter next to car keys and a
   coffee cup. Everyday, unstyled.
5. **The approval moment.** Your hand holding the phone showing an EarnIt photo
   submission with the approve button. Hand only, no face.

Rules for Set A: **crude is the point.** Slightly tilted, real surfaces, real
clutter, visible fingerprints on the screen. If it looks professionally
photographed, it will underperform. In-image words land at 4–14 for a sticker
or overlay, up to about 28 if it's handwriting.

**A format nobody in the adjacent categories is currently running:** the App
Store review card. Screenshot a real 5-star review of EarnIt once you have
one, and run it as-is. That is open space.

## Set B — the Benton Lane shots (brand surfaces)

For the site, the Facebook cover, and the organic grid, where the job is to
look like a company worth trusting rather than to stop a scroll. Reference:
Benton Lane Home and its brands (Lemon Park, Painted Paper, Anthem Classic) —
full-bleed tactile photography, warm natural light, shallow depth of field,
almost no type on the image.

6. A made bed, shot from the foot, slightly above
7. The same bed unmade, for a two-panel before/after
8. A phone face-down on a nightstand, lamp in frame
9. A cleared kitchen counter in morning light
10. A dishwasher loaded neatly, door open
11. Folded laundry stacked on a bed
12. A tidy desk, homework closed and stacked
13. A child's hands pulling a duvet straight (hands only)
14. A backpack by the front door, ready to go

Shoot each landscape and portrait. Don't tidy beyond what's real.

---

## What happens when you send them

Set A becomes 4–6 paid statics with handwriting and overlays composited in,
sized 1080×1350 with text kept off the bottom and side edges. Set B becomes the
Facebook cover, the site hero, and the organic posts in the Benton Lane
register.

Nothing here needs a photographer, a model release, or AI-generated people —
which matters, because AI-generated children in ads for a child-safety product
read as untrustworthy the moment a parent notices.

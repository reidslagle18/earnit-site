# The Benton Lane model — what they actually run

Researched 28 Jul 2026 in the Meta Ad Library, first-hand.

## The finding that matters

**Benton Lane, the parent brand, runs no Meta ads at all.** A search for the
exact phrase returns four results, all from a restaurant chain that happens to
mention a winery of the same name.

**The advertising happens under the product brands.** "The Painted Paper" is
running **~380 active ads**, continuously since January 2026 — a real,
always-on programme. Their brands are Painted Paper, Hillcroft, Anthem Classic
and Lemon Park; Benton Lane is the house that holds them.

The parent site's job is credibility for design professionals — "a curated
collection of premium home interior lines crafted for design professionals,"
members-only trade pricing, made in America, fair trade. It is a **trade/B2B
storefront**, not a consumer acquisition engine. That distinction matters for
us: their *look* is calibrated for an expert audience browsing a catalogue,
their *ads* are calibrated for cold consumers. We should copy the ads.

## Their creative model, itemised

**1. The image is the product. It carries no headline.**
Wallpaper swatches, styled room shots, hands installing a panel. No type
overlay, no logo lockup, no CTA button baked in. Meta's own chrome — headline,
description, CTA — does that work. This is the single biggest difference from
what we've been building.

**2. All the argument lives in primary text**, and the voice is short
declarative fragments in parallel. Verbatim from live ads:

> No paste. No tools. No contractor.
> Peel. Stick. Done.
> Small change. Big difference.
> No tools. No mess. No learning curve.
> Wallpaper doesn't need defending anymore.
> Nothing about this needed a plan.

That cadence is the same antithesis structure as "Screen time earned, not
given." They are already speaking our voice.

**3. Objection-handling is a whole creative angle**, not a footnote:

> This is the part everyone worries about. Watch it come down clean — no peeling paint, no mess, no repair needed.

**4. Headline field: 3–5 words.** `Peel. Stick. Done.` · `High Impact, No
Overhaul` · `Shop All Wallpaper`

**5. Description field carries trust and logistics.** `Free Shipping` ·
`USA Made` · `This Changes the Whole Room`

**6. Sale ads are the one exception** — those *do* put type on the image, set
in an ornate serif: `50% OFF WALLPAPER SAMPLES / Half priced samples + FREE
Shipping / This week only`.

**7. Volume via the variant engine.** Ad cards repeatedly read "2 ads use this
creative and text" and "12 ads use this creative and text" — one creative,
many permutations, exactly the production method the benchmark data recommends.

**8. Static and video side by side**, running on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger
and Threads simultaneously.

**9. Two audiences in one account.** Consumer ads to `/collections/shop-all-wallpaper`
and trade-program ads to `/pages/trade-sign-up`, running concurrently.

## What we change for EarnIt

The aligned version of our ad is **`c3-fullbleed`** — the phone photograph with
the real iMessage thread, filling the frame, **no headline band and no
wordmark**. The line moves off the image and into Meta's fields:

- **Primary text:** Four texts about the trash. No replies. It isn't laziness — the phone wins every time it's a fair fight. EarnIt keeps the games locked until the job is actually done.
- **Headline:** `Screen time earned, not given.`
- **Description:** `First week free`
- **CTA:** `Learn more`

Run it against `b5-aligned` (the version with the emerald headline band). Same
photograph, opposite theory of where words belong. That is a clean, honest test
and it settles the question with data rather than taste.

Keep the **offer exception**: for a sale or a free-week push, put type on the
image in Baskervville, the way they do.

# Social content for EarnIt

Their organic and paid share one asset library — the same room photography runs
in both. We should work the same way: **every ad becomes a post, every post is
shot to also work as an ad.**

Instagram: `@bentonlanehome` is their handle for reference.

## Six content pillars

**1. The thread series** — our strongest asset. One parent-and-kid exchange per
post, screenshotted the way the ads are. Infinitely extensible, deeply
shareable, and it costs nothing to produce. This is the pillar that grows the
account.
*"can you take the trash out" / "trash?" / "hello"* · *"5 more minutes" ×4* ·
*"i can't, i have to finish my chores first"*

**2. House rules** — single sentences set in Baskervville on parchment. The
"earned, not given" family lives here: *Screens follow chores. Not the other
way around.* · *Nothing on this phone is free.* · *First the work. Then the
phone.* These are save-and-share posts, and they're the cheapest thing on the
list to make.

**3. Before and after** — the kid's room, same angle, messy then tidy. This is
the direct analogue of their room photography, and it's the pillar that needs
your camera.

**4. Objection answers** — copy their playbook exactly. One post per fear:
*What happens in an emergency?* (calls are never locked) · *Can my kid delete
it?* (no) · *Is this spying?* (no messages, no location). Each one kills a
purchase objection and doubles as an ad.

**5. Product truth** — screen recordings of the real app: assigning a task,
submitting a photo, the moment apps unlock. Reels format. Shows the thing
working, which is what converts.

**6. Real approvals** — once you have users and permission, actual photo
submissions with the approval stamp. Highest-trust content there is, and it
costs nothing but consent.

## Cadence and format

Three to four posts a week is enough. Two threads, one house rule, one product
or objection post. Reels for reach, statics for saves, carousels for the
explainers ("Five chores actually worth thirty minutes").

Shoot everything **1080×1350** so a post can become an ad without a re-crop —
that's the whole point of a shared library.

## The rule that keeps it coherent

Their entire system holds together because every image is the product in a real
room, and every caption is short declarative sentences. Ours holds together if
every image is **a real phone or a real room**, and every caption is **the
earned-not-given voice**. Nothing else needs to be consistent.

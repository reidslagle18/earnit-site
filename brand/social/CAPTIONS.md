# EarnIt — eight posts, ready to publish

Images are in this folder, all 1080×1350 so any of them can also be run as an
ad without re-cropping. Post two to four a week; alternate a thread, a rule and
an objection so the grid doesn't read as all one thing.

Facebook and Instagram take the same caption. On Instagram put the hashtags at
the end; on Facebook drop them.

## s1 — "Screens follow chores. Not the other way around."

> The order matters more than the rule.
>
> When screen time comes first, chores become a negotiation. When chores come first, they're just what happens before the phone unlocks.
>
> Screen time earned, not given.

## s3 — "Nothing on this phone is free."

> Not a punishment. A price.
>
> Bed made, dishwasher emptied, twenty minutes of reading — then the apps open. Your kid knows exactly what it costs and exactly how to pay it.
>
> Screen time earned, not given.

---

## s4 — "What happens in an emergency?"

> The first question every parent asks us, and the answer is never.
>
> Phone calls, texts to you and emergency services are never locked — not while tasks are pending, not ever. EarnIt only locks games and social apps.
>
> You can also set an emergency unlock code that opens everything instantly.

---

## s5 — "Can my kid just delete it?"

> Short answer: no.
>
> Deleting the app, restarting the phone, or digging through settings doesn't lift the lock. EarnIt runs on Apple's own Family Controls — the same system behind Screen Time — so iOS enforces it, not an app your kid can force-quit.

---

## s6 — "Is this spying on my kid?"

> No. And we think that's the whole point.
>
> EarnIt never reads messages and never tracks location. It does one thing: keeps games and social apps locked until the work is done. Your kid can see exactly what's locked and exactly how to earn it back — which is why they don't hate it.

## What to make next, once you have five minutes with your phone

The two pillars I can't build without you:

**Before and after** — your kid's room from the same spot, messy then tidy.
Caption writes itself: *"Same room. Twenty minutes apart. Nobody argued."*

**Product truth** — a screen recording of assigning a task, submitting a photo,
and the apps unlocking. Post as a Reel. This is the one that converts, because
it proves the thing works.

## The rule that keeps the account coherent

Every image is a real phone or a real room. Every caption is short declarative
sentences. Sign off with **"Screen time earned, not given."** often enough that
people start associating the line with you.
